Six terms worth knowing before you read on

Skip this if you're already fluent in AI-conference jargon. Otherwise, these six terms show up repeatedly below.

Agentic AI — AI that doesn't just answer a question, but takes multi-step actions on its own toward a goal.
AX — shorthand for "AI Transformation," the enterprise-wide effort to adopt AI, not just a single tool.
Harness Engineering — the tooling and guardrails around an AI model so it can run unsupervised.
Sim-to-Real gap — when something tested in a lab/simulator fails once it meets real-world mess.
World model — an AI's internal understanding of how physical things behave.
FDE (Forward-Deployed Engineer) — a vendor's own engineer embedded inside a customer's rollout.

Day 1 — Foundations & the enterprise reality check

The first day moved from where agentic AI came from, to how far it actually reaches into production today.

Morning · Main Stage

The Long Road to Agentic AI: A Pioneer's Perspective선구자의 관점에서 본 AI 어시스턴트에서 에이전틱 AI의 시대

Larry Heck — Professor, Georgia Institute of TechnologyCareer: SRI → Nuance → Yahoo → Cortana → Google Assistant → Samsung Bixby/Viv → Georgia Tech
Highlights
  • Traced "agentic AI" back to SRI's 1995 Office Assistant — the direct ancestor of Siri and today's AI agents.
  • Argued 2010s-era assistants (Cortana, Google Assistant, Bixby) were deliberately limited, but controllable and fixable when something went wrong.
  • Called 2022's ChatGPT moment "the wrong turn": fluent, but the older, more controllable structure was thrown out along with the friction.
  • Cited 2026 research: today's agents guess when a task is unclear (89% success with a clear task vs. 6.7% with a vague one).
InsightSitting through this, what stuck with me is that the old assistants could actually explain themselves and get fixed when they got something wrong. Today's AI just sounds confident — even when it's guessing. For any chatbot or advising tool we look at, that's the real question to ask a vendor: what does it do when it's not sure? A smooth demo won't show you that; you have to ask directly.
Vision 2030 pillarFuture Ready
TakeawaySounding smart isn't the same as being reliable. Before we put any AI assistant in front of students for advising or admissions, we should make the vendor show us what happens when a student asks something confusing or off-topic — not just watch a polished demo.
Morning · Main Stage

Agentic AI in Space: Traditional and Agentic AI Are Revolutionizing Space지구 밖의 자율 AI: 60개 위성이 스스로 생각하고 협력하는 법

Steve Chien — JPL Fellow & Senior Research Scientist, NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Highlights
  • Demoed a workflow that detects an event, automatically picks the right satellites to look at it, and tracks it — shown live with a simulated Mount Pinatubo eruption.
  • Framed the goal as "humans on the loop, not in the loop" — people set intent and check results; the system does the coordination.
InsightThis works because if the system points a satellite the wrong way, you just point it again — no harm done. A school doesn't get that luxury: a misrouted student inquiry, or a wrongly flagged academic-integrity case, isn't something you just "try again." So "let AI coordinate, humans just check" only really works for the low-stakes stuff.
Vision 2030 pillarFuture Ready
TakeawayThis "set the goal, let it coordinate, check the result" pattern is worth borrowing — but only for tasks where a mistake is cheap to catch and fix, like syncing schedules between offices. Anything touching a student's record should stay more hands-on.
Morning · Main Stage

Is AI Actually Helping Enterprise Productivity?LLM & Productivity

Prof. Jun-gi Lee — Yonsei University Young-ok Kim — CAIO, HD Hyundai Jong-hwan Kim — Executive Director, Lotte Shopping Kyung-jong Lee — Executive Director, KB Kookmin Bank
Highlights
  • Of executives running multiple AI projects, only 6% said the projects moved profit by more than 2% — 94% saw no measurable financial impact.
  • A Bank of Korea survey found 60–70% of Korean companies say they're adopting AI, but fewer than 10% report actual results.
  • Gartner data: over half of enterprise AI projects get cancelled at the pilot stage.
InsightThree separate studies, same story: most companies using AI can't actually show it did anything for their numbers. That's my takeaway #2 in a nutshell — a working pilot and a working product are two different things. We should expect the same slow, unclear payoff from our own chatbot and advising pilots, and just say that plainly to the Board instead of overpromising.
Vision 2030 pillarsFinancial Growth · Future Ready
TakeawayUsing AI and AI actually helping are two different claims. Every pilot we run should track one specific thing — hours saved, faster replies, more students staying enrolled — before we call it a win or spend more on it.↳ Ties to takeaway #2: PoC ≠ production.
Midday · Main Stage

2026 Enterprise Agentic AX Technology Trends: Harness & Loop Engineering2026 Enterprise Agentic AX 기술 트렌드 및 Harness & Loop 엔지니어링 적용 사례 소개

Hun-dong Kim — Architect Manager, Microsoft
Highlights
  • Described a maturity ladder — casual chatbot use, then developer tools, then multi-tool platforms, then fully governed enterprise setups.
  • Explained "Loop Engineering": set a goal, let the agent try, have it check its own work, fix on failure, stop on success.
  • Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS, and Microsoft have together committed over $9 billion to embedding their own staff inside customers' AI rollouts.
InsightEvery big AI company is now doing the same thing: sending their own people to actually help clients set things up, not just selling the software. That told me picking an AI isn't the hard part anymore — getting it running properly is. When we talk to vendors, we should be asking what hands-on help comes with the contract, not just what the AI can do.
Vision 2030 pillarsFuture Ready · Global Partnership Engagement
TakeawayAsk every vendor what real setup help they'll give us — most of the big names now offer it. Building a demo is easy; getting it actually running for the whole school is the real work.↳ Ties to takeaway #3: Building is easy. Scaling is the real work.
Afternoon · Main Stage

The Agentic Data Stack: Speed and Simplicity for AI That Scales

Ki-hoon Lee — Solutions Architect, ClickHouse
Highlights
  • AI agent queries shifted from one predictable request per user to many unpredictable requests at once.
  • OpenAI's daily logs compare to "500 Libraries of Congress a day"; Anthropic credited this tech in shipping Claude 4.
InsightAn AI tool hits a database very differently than a normal app — lots of requests firing off at once, not one at a time. If a lot of students use our chatbot or advising tool together, our current systems could slow down or break in ways nobody tested for. That's a data problem before it's ever a model problem.
Vision 2030 pillarFuture Ready
TakeawayBefore we pick a platform, IT should check if our systems can actually handle a lot of students using an AI tool at the same time — not just one person testing it quietly.↳ Ties to takeaway #1: Data is the bottleneck, not the model.
Afternoon · Main Stage

From Models to Reality: What It Takes to Run AI Reliably at Scale모델에서 현실까지: 대규모 AI를 안정적으로 운영하기 위한 조건

Colin Presly · VP, Customer Engineering, Seagate Technology
Highlights
  • Three stages: can we build something useful → can we run it in production → does it create lasting value. Most stay stuck between the first two.
  • 85–95% of all AI data ultimately rests on ordinary hard drives, not premium chips.
InsightIt's easy to forget, but every AI tool has to keep its data somewhere, and that costs money for years, not just once. Yes, the speaker sells storage — but the point held up: most of what a school ends up paying for AI comes down to where the data lives and how long we keep it, not the flashy part.
Vision 2030 pillarsFinancial Growth · Future Ready
TakeawayBefore we spend more on AI, Finance and IT should figure out where all this student data will actually live, and what that costs us over time — not just the price tag on the AI tool itself.↳ Ties to takeaway #10: infrastructure wins the gold rush.
Afternoon · Main Stage

How AI Is Redefining the Future of WorkAI가 바꾸는 일의 미래

Ryan Kang — CEO, HP Korea
Highlights
  • Roughly 30% of the global office workforce is now Gen Z, with higher entrepreneurial instincts than earlier generations.
InsightThis talk was about the workforce, but it made me think about our own students instead. Gen Z already grew up using AI. Our incoming students are the same — they'll already know these tools before they even enroll. So our AI training shouldn't just be for staff; the students are already ahead of us on this one.
Vision 2030 pillarsEnrollment Growth · Future Ready

Evening · Main Stage

The Rise of AI-Native WorkAI 네이티브 워크플로우 혁명

Laurent Thevenet — Head of Creative Technology, APAC & MEA, Publicis Groupe
Highlights
  • "Instant capability isn't expertise" — the gap between fast AI output and knowing what's actually good is "slop."
  • Ownership rule: 5 users, the builder owns it; 50, needs a named owner; 500, needs a full team.
InsightThe rule was: 5 users, one person owns it casually; 50, it needs a real owner; 500, it needs a team. What I'd add is that it's less about headcount and more about how much damage a mistake could do. One teacher using an AI grading tool casually is fine; the same tool deciding grades department-wide is a different animal entirely.

Four ideas that kept coming back

01

Most AI pilots die before real use — and it's rarely the AI's fault

Messy data, tools bolted onto old workflows, and hesitant leadership were named as the real blockers across five separate sessions on three different tracks.

02

The hard part moved from "which model" to "how do we actually run this"

The $9B+ race among major vendors to embed engineers in customer rollouts shows picking a model is the easy part now.

03

Physical AI needs a stricter safety bar than software AI

The Physical AI panel's "can it be undone?" rule echoed across the manufacturing panel and Seagate's storage-reliability framing.

04

Good data is the prerequisite everyone agreed on, independently

ClickHouse, Seagate, DeepMind, Cisco, and the manufacturing panel all named the same prerequisite in different words.

05

Vision 2030 Alignment: Who Makes This Real

The ideas above are only useful if someone owns turning them into action. This maps the summit's themes onto the five strategic pillars — with a specific office named against each recommendation, because the summit's own most-repeated failure mode was exactly this: no one senior owning the decision to act.

1 · Enrollment Growth

Give the Recruitment Chatbot persistent context across a prospect's full journey. Apply the reversibility test to the Academic Advising Agent now, while it's still in development.

Responsible — Admissions for the Recruitment Chatbot; CET with Academic Council for the Advising Agent's guardrails.
Potential ROI
  • Lower cost per enrolled student — a chatbot that keeps context across the funnel should convert more inquiries without more staff.
  • Retained tuition revenue from students the Advising Agent flags early enough to keep enrolled.
KPIs to track
  • Chatbot inquiry → completed application conversion rate
  • Average response time to a prospective student inquiry
  • Term-to-term retention rate among students the Advising Agent flagged as at-risk

2 · Enhanced Reputation and Branding

Publish a first AI case study once Phase 2 pilot results land. Brand Benilde's public AI position around governance discipline, not raw capability.

Responsible — Marketing & Communications, with CET for content; Office of the Chancellor for public positioning.
Potential ROI

AI Implementation: Roles & Responsibilities

The summit's clearest lesson was that roles without a decision path just recreate the "ad hoc committee" problem. Below is a proposed model for who does what, and who ultimately decides.

1. Digital Transformation Office (Program Lead)

The non-academic operating unit that embeds AI into the institution's digital roadmap — the orchestrator, not the builder of everything.

  • Owns the roadmap's cadence and the master pilot list — the one place that can see across every office running an AI pilot at once, not just CET's.
  • Runs the three-question go/no-go gate on every pilot before it's proposed for scale-up, regardless of which office originated it.
  • Prepares Board/BLT reporting on both efficiency and effectiveness, not adoption numbers alone.
  • Escalates anything hitting the reversibility line to Leadership — does not decide it alone.

2. IT Team

The infrastructure and safety net.

  • Owns the data-warehouse / agentic-query-capacity question before any platform is selected college-wide.
  • Runs security review on every AI vendor: data residency, access scope, breach exposure — the same review whether the pilot came from CET, HR, or Finance.
  • Owns uptime and support once a pilot graduates into daily operations; also the domain owner for its own tools (e.g. the helpdesk agentic-resolution build).

3. Center of Educational Technology (CET)

The proponent for teaching, learning, and advising AI specifically — not AI institution-wide.

  • Already the institution's AI-policy author (the DLS-CSB AI Usage Policy carries CET's letterhead) — so it's the natural proponent for anything the policy's own purpose covers: teaching and learning.
  • Vets tools against the EdTech AI Tools Guide rubric before anything reaches a classroom or advisor.
  • Runs the pilots inside its actual mandate: syllabus AI, the Academic Advising Agent, the Mental Health Agent.
  • Owns faculty and student training under the AI Literacy Framework.
  • Out of scope for CET: Bursar reconciliation, HR screening, Procurement, Alumni engagement, and similar — those belong to the offices that already own them (see Role 5).

4. Leadership (VP for Admin & Innovation, Office of the Chancellor, BLT)

The risk owner and budget releaser — the "who presses the button" role the summit's most-repeated failure mode says usually goes unfilled.

  • Signs off on anything budget-significant or reputationally sensitive.
  • Releases Phase 3 scale-up funding in gated tranches tied to Phase 2 evidence.
  • Sets the non-negotiable boundary: which decisions, per the reversibility test, never become AI's call.

5. Domain Offices (the actual proponent for anything outside teaching & learning)

Each office runs its own pilot — CET is not the default owner of every AI initiative, just the education-specific ones.

  • IT: helpdesk agentic resolution.
  • HR: resume screening assist.
  • Finance/Bursar: invoice & receipt reconciliation.
  • Registrar: document processing (with IT).
  • Library: research-assistant layer (with CET, since it touches learning support).
  • Procurement: RFP/bid summarization.
  • Marketing & Communications: content first-drafts, and the public "governance discipline" narrative.
  • Alumni Relations/Advancement: donor engagement assistant (with Marcom).
  • Admissions & Finance jointly: predictive enrollment/capacity planning.
  • Academic Council: policy guardrails for anything touching grading, advising, or academic integrity, regardless of which office built the tool.
  • Student Affairs: co-owns the Mental Health Agent given its stakes.
The decision pathThe domain office that actually owns the process runs the pilot (CET for teaching/learning/advising tools; IT, HR, Finance, Registrar, Procurement, Marcom, Alumni Relations, or Admissions for their own operational tools) → Digital Transformation Office runs the go/no-go gate → IT signs off on infrastructure/security → Leadership approves scale + budget → the domain office owns rollout. Any step that hits the reversibility line routes to Leadership regardless of how well the pilot tested.

Peer Benchmarks: What Other Schools Are Already Doing

These are named, sourced examples — not summit talk, but documented institutional moves already underway at other universities. Each is linked below so it can be independently verified before it's cited externally.

Arizona State University × OpenAI

Global Partnership Engagement

ASU became the first university with an institutional-level OpenAI partnership in January 2024, and has since expanded it to give every student, faculty, and staff member free access to ChatGPT Edu with GPT‑5. Nearly 700 projects have come out of its internal "AI Innovation Challenge," with more than 175 proposals submitted in the first two weeks alone. This is the most direct precedent for what a bundled, vendor-supported campus-wide rollout can look like at scale.

Source — ASU Enterprise Technology, "ASU and OpenAI expand collaboration"

National University of Singapore × OpenAI

Global Partnership Engagement

NUS announced a strategic OpenAI collaboration rolling out ChatGPT Edu to all students, faculty, and staff starting August 31, 2026, alongside new curriculum requirements and research platforms. As the closest Asia-Pacific peer example to what Benilde is weighing for its Phase 2 platform decision, it's a useful reference point for contract terms and rollout sequencing.

Source — NUS News, "NUS powers education, research and administration... through a strategic collaboration with OpenAI"

Georgia Institute of Technology — "Jill Watson"

Future Ready

Georgia Tech's long-running AI teaching-assistant project, running since 2016 and now rebuilt on ChatGPT, is recognized as the first documented chatbot to enhance teaching presence in online learning; IDC has separately named Georgia Tech a national leader in higher-ed AI deployment. Worth noting: Georgia Tech's AVA Lab is also where the "sidecar controller" research referenced in this summit's opening keynote (Day 1, Session 1) comes from — the same lab, two different angles on the same problem.

Source — Georgia Tech AI, news & recognition

Saudi Electronic University — academic advising chatbot

Enrollment Growth

A peer-reviewed, published deployment of an AI academic-advising chatbot, evaluated against student satisfaction, advisor workload reduction, and chatbot accuracy — a directly comparable, evidence-based precedent for Benilde's own Academic Advising Agent pilot, including the kind of measurement framework worth adapting.

Source — Journal of Computer Science, "Chatbot for Academic Advising: Case Study at Saudi Electronic University"

Philippine higher-ed AI governance landscape — Benilde already stands out

Enhanced Reputation and Branding

Peer-reviewed comparative research found at least eight Philippine universities have issued formal AI governance guidelines. Most of them — UP, UPOU, Ateneo de Manila, DLSU, and FEU — use a "soft," principle-based approach. Benilde is specifically identified in the literature as the outlier with a "hybrid" model, combining policy-driven requirements with maintained flexibility. This isn't a summit claim or an internal assessment — it's an externally published finding, which makes it a genuinely strong, citable line for Benilde's public AI narrative (see Pillar 2 above) rather than something the institution is only saying about itself.

Source — Toquero, "Trends, Trajectories and Tomorrows of AI Regulations in Philippine Higher Educational Institutions," Higher Education Quarterly (2026); corroborated by comparative analyses on ResearchGate / Zenodo.
